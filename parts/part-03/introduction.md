# Part III
# Useful Catalogs and Real Forms

![Editorial illustration: A phone and tablet present book, music and podcast catalog cards beside an open book, record sleeve, blank request form and pencil.](../../assets/illustrations/part-03-catalogs-forms.jpg)

A usable catalog needs consistent meaning as well as attractive cards. This Part develops the data behind the interface and then uses the same separation of rules and presentation to build a custom-song request.

Chapter 12 adds stable creator relationships and optional metadata to the bundled catalog. It distinguishes unavailable content from missing information and validates related fixtures before the browser receives them.

Chapter 13 extends search, filters, sorting, and favorites, with bounded local persistence and controlled typing updates. Chapter 14 develops content-specific details, reading samples, and external-link behavior. Chapter 15 builds the request form, including validation and draft ownership. Chapter 16 connects the archived pricing rules to a simulated reliable submission contract.

The Part III implementation reaches Chapter 16. Request drafts are saved only by explicit action in anonymous local storage, and quote acceptance is simulated in process memory. No deployed customer backend, checkout, or live charging is introduced. Device verification remains distinct from portable tests and compilation.

The same rule applies throughout: portable tests establish deterministic behavior; native interaction and real service integration require their own evidence. A well-formed catalog is the starting point for those experiences.
